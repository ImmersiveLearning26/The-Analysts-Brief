The Analyst’s Brief: Simulation Script and Storyboard
IRS 103 Introduction to African Politics ·

Simulation summary and learning objectives
You are a junior political analyst on the research unit of the National Dialogue Commission
of Kalinda, a fictional African republic in crisis: a disputed election, identity-based clashes
in the border town of Tamarind Crossing, rumours of an army mutiny and a corruption
scandal. In one working week, you do what political analysts do in real life. You compile and
check the background evidence, test the competing explanations for how Kalinda got here,
appraise options for responding to today’s dispute, and brief the Chair. You grew up near
the Crossing, and Dr Diallo asks you to note that as an interest in your file. Your work
shapes how the Dialogue frames the crisis.
Purpose: to develop the ability to carry out the core tasks of political analysis (sourcing
evidence, testing explanations, appraising policy options and briefing a decision-maker).
Developer creativity: the look of the research unit and the tools, period sound, transitions
and the visual treatment of the Crossing, provided facts, rules and branch logic do not
change.

Each strand ends as Strong, Mixed or Fragile. Nothing is scored in numbers on screen. The
oral briefing in S4 is unscored.
Running variables (shown as words on the heads-up display): Political climate
(Calm/Strained/Hostile), set from Strands 1 and 2 · Time budget (set in S3).

and findings submitted, response levers and rationale, and the recorded briefing. Used only
to tailor the debrief.

Outcome rule (apply in order; first match wins). The Party system and Succession tests
below use the factor’s state: F3 is “one-party” unless switched; F6 is “leader chooses”
unless switched.
  1.​    M is −2 or less → Coup
  2.​    M = −1 and (L is 0 or less, or I is 0 or less) → Coup

7 · Debrief & Leaderboard
  •​   How strands combine: there is no numeric score and no leaderboard. Each strand
       ends Strong, Mixed or Fragile (S1, S2, S3). The number of Strong strands decides the
       forward view: three = F1, two = F2, one or none = F3. The briefing in S4 is unscored.
  •​   Worked example: in S1 the learner places all sixteen evidence pieces and sets both
       claims aside → Strand 1 Strong. In S2 they switch F2 and F6 and run the model
       (outcome: Personalised rule), then add F3 (outcome: Consolidation), switch F7 and
       see no change, and send findings F2, F3 and F6 with no diversity → A is selected and
       B is 2, Dv not selected → Strand 2 Strong. The Political climate is Calm. In S3 they set
       L1-2, L2-2, L3-3, L4-2 and L5-2 (cost 1+2+2+2+2 = 9; budget 8+1 = 9, not
       overstretched). EC = 2+0+1+1+0 = 4 High; P = 0+2+1+0+0 = 3 High; PC =
       0+1+1+1+1 = 4 High; T = 0+0+1+0+2 = 3 Easing. No Low or Rising → Strand 3
       Strong. Three Strong strands → F1.
  •​   Displayed but not scored: the real-world record, the reflection prompts, the
       rationale, the briefing and its check, the number of tests run.
  •​   Assessment dimensions (formative): sourcing and evidence use (S1); causal
       reasoning and testing claims (S2); options appraisal and justification (S3); clear,
       honest communication (S4). If a grade is needed later, assess the reasoning and
       justification, not a single fixed answer.
  •​   Backend (Supabase or Firebase): student ID, session ID, timestamps per scene,
       pieces placed and claims set aside, every model run and the findings, lever settings
       and every test with indicator values, rationale, reflection responses and the briefing
       (audio stored only with the learner’s consent, with a typed alternative),
       forward-view code. Facilitator dashboard: cohort distribution of strands and
       forward views; the most common claim placed; the most-tested factors and levers;
       completion rates. No individual ranking.

9 · Branching map and edge cases
  •​   S1 → S2 (single path; Strand 1 is Strong, Mixed or Fragile).
  •​   S2: all 128 switch combinations resolve to Coup, Personalised rule or Consolidation;
       the findings set Strand 2 and the Political climate → S3.
  •​   S3: 243 lever combinations resolve to Strong, Mixed or Fragile → S4.
  •​   S4 → End / Restart.
Every screen is reachable, every path ends in a defined outcome, and no path is a dead end.
A weak finding or a rejected package is a consequence, not a failure screen.
Edge cases: “Open the case notes”, “Send findings”, “Submit to the Dialogue” and “Send to
the Chair” stay inactive until their conditions are met, with an inline tooltip explaining why.
A piece that does not fit a place bounces back. The model and the sandbox allow unlimited
runs and keep a history. A learner who sends findings without running the model is asked
once to run it; they may proceed. If the microphone is unavailable or declined, the typed
briefing is offered. Global Undo reverses unsubmitted choices only; submitted decisions
stand. No timers, so doing nothing leaves the story waiting.
